The broth decides when we close.

Every morning at 5:00, Mariko starts the pot: pork femur, back fat, nothing else, at a rolling boil hard enough to emulsify the marrow into the water. Fourteen hours later it turns the color of light through a whiskey glass. That's the whole recipe. There is no backup batch.

Most nights the pot holds about ninety bowls. Fridays it can run to a hundred and ten. When the ladle scrapes bottom we hang the wooden sign, bow to whoever's still in line, and start again tomorrow. We'd rather disappoint you at the door than dilute a bowl.

Eighteen stools, one counter, zero table service.

You'll queue, you'll sit elbow to elbow with a stranger, and you'll probably leave with a new favorite person. The counter is cypress, the stools wobble slightly, and the ticket is cash or card but never an app.

  • Line etiquette: hold your spot in person — no placeholders, no holding six seats for friends who are "five minutes away."
  • Solo diners almost always get in within twenty minutes. Parties of four or more should expect to split.
  • Eat, then go. We love you, but someone behind you is cold. Lingering is for the bar two doors down.
