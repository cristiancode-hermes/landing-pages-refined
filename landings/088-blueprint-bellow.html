The weekend, hour by hour

No lecture hall, no slideshow. You're at the anvil by 9:20 on Saturday morning and your forearms will know about it by lunch.

Day one — Saturday, 9:00–16:30

Fire and basics

  1. 9:00
    Light the forge yourself. Coke, air, patience. If it won't catch, that's the first lesson.
  2. 10:15
    Drawing out. Turn a square bar into a taper. This is 80% of everything a smith does.
  3. 12:30
    Lunch. Stew from the pub next door, eaten with black hands. Nobody minds.
  4. 13:15
    Scrolls and twists. The pretty stuff. Your S-hook starts looking like something.
  5. 15:45
    Quench and finish. Wire brush, wax, and the first thing you ever forged goes in your bag.

Day two — Sunday, 9:00–16:00

The rail-spike knife

  1. 9:00
    Steel talk. Why a rail spike, what carbon does, when to harden and when to leave it soft.
  2. 9:45
    Forge the blade. Flatten, draw the point, shape the edge. Expect to re-do the tip once.
  3. 12:30
    Lunch. Compare blisters. Sunday's are always worse.
  4. 13:15
    Quench the edge. The hiss, the steam, the moment it becomes a knife. You'll remember this.
  5. 14:30
    Grind, sharpen, strop. Then cut the rope. If it slices clean, you pass. It will.

The honesty waiver

You will get soot in places soot should not be. Your forearms will remember Sunday until about Thursday. One student in four raises a blister; one in ten singes an eyebrow leaning in to look at the color.

There is real fire and there are real knives. We provide aprons, gloves and goggles, and we watch you like hawks — but you are working a forge at 1,100 degrees, not sitting in a paint-and-sip. If that sentence worried you, this is not your weekend. If it made you grin, sign here.

Everyone signs the same waiver our instructors signed. Nobody is exempt, including the two of us.

— Ruth Okafor & Dario Bell, smiths and proprietors

What you make, you keep

Everything you forge goes home with you. Crooked, proud, yours.

  • The S-hook Saturday

    Your first taper, your first scroll. Hang a plant on it for the next thirty years.

  • The bottle opener Saturday, if the hook behaves

    Punched and drifted from bar stock. It will outlive every bottle it opens.

  • The rail-spike knife Sunday

    A real edge, hardened and sharpened by you. People will ask where you bought it. Correct them loudly.
