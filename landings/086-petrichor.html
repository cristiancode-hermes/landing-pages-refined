The geosmin story.

Geosmin — "earth smell" in Greek — is made by soil bacteria. Your nose detects it at five parts per trillion, which is like tasting a teaspoon of sugar dissolved in four Olympic pools. It is why a garden after rain smells alive and a parking garage after rain smells like a cave.

Natural extraction yields almost nothing, so ours is fermented by yeast in a lab outside Groningen, identical to what the bacteria make. The honesty section: geosmin alone smells like a basement. The violet leaf and slate are what move it from cellar to storm.

55 hours

Burn time, honestly.

We say 55 hours because we timed it — eleven candles, first light to self-extinguish, in a 19°C room. Your mileage varies with drafts and how religiously you trim the wick. Anyone claiming 80 hours from this much wax is rounding to the nearest fantasy.

First burn matters

The two rules.

One: the first burn must reach the edges — about three hours — or the wax tunnels and you lose a third of the candle. Two: trim the wick before every relight. That's the whole maintenance manual. If your candle misbehaves despite both, email us a photo and we replace it, no forms.
