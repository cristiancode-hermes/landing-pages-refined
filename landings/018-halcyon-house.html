A twelve-room house above Dryopida Bay

Do very little, beautifully.

No schedule, no scene, no lobby music. Breakfast when you wake, the sea when you want it, and a house that keeps out of your way.

12 rooms May – October Adults only

White plaster, one blue, and nothing asking for your attention.

Halcyon House sits on the quiet side of the island — the side the ferry schedule protects. A converted shipowner’s house from 1894, kept deliberately small.

Days here have a shape but not a timetable. Coffee appears on the terrace from seven until eleven. The pool is unheated and unbothered. Dinner is served when the boats come in, not before. If you need something, Katarina is at the desk; if you don’t, she isn’t.

The table

One seating, one short menu, whatever the boats brought. Nikos cooks; the sea decides.

Dinner is for house guests first — the remaining four tables go to the island at noon each day. Lunch is a plate of something cold, a tomato, and bread, served under the fig tree from one o’clock.

Vegetarians are fed properly here, not apologetically. Tell us at breakfast.

The island, if you must

Most guests mean to explore and don’t. This is a success, not a failure.

Should restlessness strike: the coast path runs eleven kilometres past three swimmable coves and one chapel. The harbor is a twenty-minute walk for the evening promenade, and Katarina’s brother runs the taxi boat to the blue caves on calm mornings.

We keep a shelf of paperbacks in five languages and a honesty bar that closes when the last guest goes up. That is the entire activities program.

The season runs May to October. The quiet rooms go first.
