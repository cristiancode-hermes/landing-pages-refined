We don't do choppers.

Nothing against them — some are beautiful. But a raked-out frame and a sprung saddle is a different religion, and we'd be learning on your money. We restore bikes to how the factory made them, or how the factory should have. That's the whole craft, and we're still getting better at it.

Same goes for full custom builds, cafe conversions of rare bikes, and anything involving a rattle can. If that's your project, we'll happily point you at three shops who do it well. Ask Ray — he keeps a list and strong opinions.

What we will always take: the unglamorous stuff. The commuter 250 with a dead battery. The barn find that turns out to be mostly barn. Nobody's bike is too ordinary to be saved.

Parts sourcing

Half of restoration is detective work. We keep a network of breakers, dealers' old stock, and machinists across four countries — and a card index older than most of the staff.

NOS & used-good

New-old-stock when it exists, inspected used parts when it doesn't. Every used part is photographed and graded before you pay. If we wouldn't bolt it to our own bike, it doesn't ship.

Machined in-house

Bushes, spacers, valve guides, and the small obscure bits that stopped existing in 1974. Our lathe is from 1962 and holds tolerance better than the new one did.

The wanted list

Can't find it? It goes on the wanted board with your name and phone number. Average wait last year: 11 weeks. Longest running entry: a 1954 velocette fishtail silencer, six years. We're still looking, Alan.

Book the bench

Bench slots open three weeks out. Bring the bike, the logbook, and whatever history you've got — even the napkin the last owner wrote the timing on. Especially that napkin.
