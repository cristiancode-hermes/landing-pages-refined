Eighty years on the same strings

Elsa Voronova opened The Marionette in 1946 with two suitcases of puppets she’d carried out of Prague. The theater has burned once, flooded twice, and never missed a winter season.

“The puppet doesn’t pretend to be alive. It asks you to lend it your aliveness — and audiences, bless them, always pay up.”
— Elsa Voronova, founder, interviewed in 1978
  1. 1946

    Elsa stages the first show — The Firebird — for forty neighbors in the Lyric Hall’s unheated back room.

  2. 1963

    The great fire takes the stage and sixty puppets. The company performs in a tent for two seasons while the hall is rebuilt.

  3. 1988

    The apprentice program begins: a two-year bench education in carving, stringing, and voicing. Forty-one puppeteers have graduated since.

  4. 2011

    The clockwork garden set debuts — 300 moving parts, zero motors, all cranked by hand from beneath the stage.

  5. 2026

    The 80th season. Third-generation artistic director Mara Voronova-Hale still carves the leads herself.

Come behind the curtain

The theater is smaller than its reputation and friendlier than its facade. Here is how to get inside.

Workshop Saturdays

Carve and string your own rod puppet with our bench masters. Three hours, all tools and linden wood provided, tea from the prop kitchen. Ages seven and up — adults unaccompanied by children are not only welcome but common.

Saturdays 10 AM – 1 PM · $35, materials included · 14 benches, booking advised

School matinees

Tuesday mornings we play for school groups, with a backstage walk after the show and a study guide teachers actually use. Title I schools ride free on the Voronova fund.

Tuesdays 11 AM · $6 per student, teachers free · call two weeks ahead
