Technical specifications

From the 1992 press kit. All figures verified by the marketing department, which was also the engineering department, which was Gary.

PROCESSOR
Twin 8-bit hearts running at 7.16 MHz. Overclockable to 7.17 MHz by asking nicely.
MEMORY
512 KB of RAM. None of it used correctly, all of it used confidently.
GRAPHICS
16.8 million colors on screen simultaneously. Four of them good. Sprite count: unlimited, in the sense that we never shipped a limit.
SOUND
6-channel FM synthesis by the legendary YM-something. Sounds like a saxophone falling down the stairs, in a good way.
STORAGE
Cartridges up to 48 Mbit. Battery-backed saves that outlast the company by 31 years and counting.
CONTROLLER
Nine buttons, one of them unlabeled. The manual says "you'll know when."
BLAST PROCESSING
Adjacent. Legally we must say adjacent.

The lore

An honest account of the Vaporware VX-1, compiled from court documents, one surviving dev kit, and Gary.

1989

Announced at a trade show the company was not invited to. The prototype was a VCR in a nicer shell, but the render looked incredible, and four magazines put it on the cover.

1991

CES debut. The demo unit played exactly one game for eleven seconds before a man in a suit would reset it. Pre-orders opened anyway. 40,000 people paid a deposit. The deposit bought a very good trade-show banner.

1993

Cancelled on March 14, eleven days before launch, citing "a desire to get it right." The warehouse of finished consoles was later found to contain 12,000 empty boxes and one very confused forklift driver.

2024

A working dev kit surfaces in a storage unit in Reno. It boots. It plays Sunset Hyperdrive for eleven seconds. It was worth it. This site is maintained by the people who found it, out of respect.
