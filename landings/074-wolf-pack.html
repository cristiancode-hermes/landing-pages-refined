Run all night. Finish to soup.

The Wolf Pack 100 is a point-to-point footrace over Kestrel Ridge, run almost entirely in the dark, by headlamp, with the wolves for company. Three distances. One cutoff. No pacers, no crews, no excuses.

Register — $145 See the course

The ridge, honest

One hundred kilometers, 4,180 meters of climbing, and a high point of 2,214 m at The Howl — which you hit at roughly 3 a.m., sixty kilometers in, when the temperature drops to single digits. The profile below draws as you scroll. Lanterns mark the aid stations.

Mandatory kit — checked twice

Checked at bib pickup and again at Low Creek (km 82). Missing item at the second check means your race ends there, warm, fed, and officially DNF.

  • Headlamp, 300+ lumens, plus spare batteriesYour backup lamp counts as your spare batteries' best friend. Bring it too.
  • Waterproof jacket, taped seamsNot a windbreaker. We check the seams. We have opinions.
  • Insulated layer, 200 g minimum fill or equivalentThe Howl at 3 a.m. has ended races for people in hoodies.
  • 1.5 L water capacity + 600 kcal of foodAid stations are 19 km apart on average. That's a long way to bonk.
  • Emergency blanket + whistleWeighs 60 grams. Has saved three racers in nine years.

The aid-station menu

Run by 60 volunteers from the valley, several of whom have been feeding racers since before you owned trail shoes. All stations have water, electrolyte, and someone who will tell you the truth about how you look.

  • Pinchot · km 22 · 01:30-ishSalted potatoes, cola, banana halves, strong coffee, weaker advice
  • Saddle · km 41 · 03:45-ishCheese quesadillas, broth, gummy bears, a heater that mostly works
  • The Howl · km 60 · dawnHot chocolate, bacon, dry socks from the goodwill bin, the sunrise
  • Low Creek · km 82 · morningRice balls, pickle juice, watermelon, honest time-checks to the cutoff

The finish-line soup story

In 2017, race director Marta Voss promised her grandmother's lentil soup to anyone who finished before the cutoff. Eleven people did. She made twelve liters and cried anyway.

Last year 214 finishers crossed the line and every one of them got a bowl — lentil, still her grandmother's recipe, still made by Marta, now with four helpers and a second stove. The soup has never run out. Marta says it never will. The wolves, presumably, are watching.

— Marta Voss, RD, finisher of all nine editions
