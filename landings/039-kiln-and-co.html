The community kiln keeps its own calendar.

Our two kilns — Brünhilde (electric, cone 6) and Old Faithful (gas, cone 10 reduction) — fire on a fixed weekly rhythm. Your work goes in when it's bone dry and comes out when the kiln says so, not before. We've never once opened early and had it go well.

Community firing is open to non-members: bring finished, bone-dry work and we charge $4 per kilogram of fired weight, glazed or bisque. We measure, you pay, Brünhilde decides.

Seconds sale, first Saturday. We tell you exactly what's wrong.

Every first Saturday, 9 am to noon, the trestle tables come out and so do the pieces that didn't make the shelf: $5–$25 each, cash or card, priced by how much we wince.

Nothing in the sale is dangerous. But some of it is weird, and we'd rather point at the weirdness than let you find it at home. Every piece carries a tag naming its flaw, written by the person who made it.

The flaw glossary, straight from the tags

Pinholed glaze
Tiny craters where gas escaped. Cosmetic only — the piece is fully vitrified and food-safe.
Warped rim
It leans. Holds liquid perfectly; looks like it's listening to you.
Crawled glaze
Bare patches where the glaze pulled away in the firing. Sealed with a food-safe touch-up or sold as a planter.
S-crack (repaired)
We don't sell these for food use, ever. Repaired ones become pencil cups and are tagged as such.

If a flaw turns out worse than the tag said, bring it back. Full refund, no receipt needed — we remember our own work.
