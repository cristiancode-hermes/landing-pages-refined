The Vetra Ballroom, Clerkenwell — one night only, four hundred masks, no exceptions.

Claim a mask Read the decree

One night a year, the city wears gold

Doors at nine. Candles, not chandeliers — eight hundred of them, lit by hand from six. Coats are taken, names are not. You are who your mask says you are until the toast.

The Decree of Dress

Issued by the hosts and enforced, cheerfully but absolutely, at the door.

  • Masks are not optional.Black, gold, or bone-white. A limited number of house masks wait at the door for the forgetful — £20, donated to the night shelter.
  • Evening dress, full length.Black tie at minimum. Gloves encouraged. Trainers will be admired, then turned away.
  • No phones on the ballroom floor.A photographer roams so you don't have to. Your likeness arrives by post, printed, within the fortnight.

The Midnight Toast

A fourteen-piece orchestra plays waltzes until the clock is done with eleven.

At midnight the music stops mid-phrase. Four hundred glasses of the 2015 are raised to whoever you decided to be tonight — and then, masks off. What happens after the toast is between you and the waltz.

Tickets vanish at capacity

Four hundred tickets exist. 136 remain. There is no waitlist, and there is no next month.
