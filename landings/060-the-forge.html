The no-experience truth

Every bootcamp says "no experience required." Here's what we actually mean — and what it costs you.

You don't need a degree.

71% of our graduates never finished a CS program. One was a long-haul trucker. One was a pastry chef. Both deploy to production on weekdays now.

You do need the hours.

This is 50+ hours a week for sixteen weeks. If you can only give it evenings, don't apply — we'd rather lose your tuition than waste four months of your life.

We won't call it easy.

Week six breaks almost everyone. That's by design: you hit the wall here, with coaches and cohort around you, instead of alone on the job.

The syllabus, week by week

No "modules," no mystery. This is exactly what you'll do, in the order you'll do it.

01

Weeks 1–2 — Foundations, for real

Terminal, Git, how the web actually moves bytes. You'll break a repo on purpose and fix it, so nothing on a computer ever feels like magic again.

02

Weeks 3–5 — JavaScript, no crutches

Closures, the event loop, async, data structures — before any framework touches your hands. Three weeks of reps until the syntax disappears.

03

Weeks 6–8 — The backend

Node, SQL, REST, auth. You'll build an API, watch it fall over under load, and learn why. This is the wall. We climb it in pairs.

04

Weeks 9–10 — Frontend that holds up

React, state, accessibility, and testing — because "it worked on my machine" is not a defense. Every component you ship gets tested and read aloud in review.

05

Weeks 11–12 — Team sprint

Four-person teams, a real brief from a real local business, standups at 9 sharp, a demo at the end that the client actually uses.

06

Weeks 13–14 — Capstone

Your project, your stack, our coaches on tap. It must be deployed, tested, and demo-able by strangers. Half of last cohort's job offers traced back to capstone demos.

07

Week 15 — The interview forge

Whiteboards, take-homes, salary talks — rehearsed with engineers who interview for a living. You'll fail three mock interviews here so you don't fail real ones later.

08

Week 16 — Demo day

You present to 40+ hiring partners with open reqs. We make the introductions. Then you do the thing you've done all along: show them working code.

Tuition, no fine print

Two ways to pay. We'll tell you which one we'd pick — even when it's not the one that earns us more.

If you can pay upfront, do. It's the cheaper path and we'd tell your own sister the same.

Income share

12% × 24 mo

  • Nothing due until you're hired at $50k+
