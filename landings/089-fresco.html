Why lime breathes.

A lime wall is not a coating; it's a slow chemical round-trip. The limestone was burned into quicklime, slaked with water into putty, and aged in pits at our works for thirty-six months. On your wall it does one last thing: it drinks CO₂ back out of the air and turns into limestone again.

Because it cures as a mineral — not a plastic film — water vapor passes straight through. Damp evaporates outward instead of blistering paint. Old buildings with lime walls simply don't peel; they mellow.
