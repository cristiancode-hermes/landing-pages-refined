How we know what we know

Every number on this page traces back to a sensor, a satellite pass, or a person with a measuring stick. Here is the chain.

Gauges

Pressure transducers bolted to bridge pilings and gauge houses, solar-powered, checked by hand twice a year. When one drifts more than 2 cm, we pull it and say so in the changelog.

Satellites

Radar altimetry and optical flood extent from public constellations, cross-checked against the gauges. Satellites see the big picture; gauges keep them honest.

Community reports

4,100 trained volunteers photograph staff gauges and high-water marks after every event. Their readings settle arguments the instruments can't.

The annual report, including the misses

An honest report has a "what went wrong" chapter. Ours is chapter four. In FY25 we over-promised sensor uptime in the Mekong pilot (94% promised, 89% delivered — monsoon flooding took out 6 units before we raised the mounts), and our first SMS vendor dropped 3% of alerts during the July crest. We switched vendors in August and published the postmortem.

Everything below is from the audited FY25 statements, which we publish in full, unedited, every January.

Hold the stick. Read the water.

Our 4,100 volunteers are the third instrument in the network. After a flood, you walk your assigned reach with a staff gauge and a phone, photograph the high-water marks, and upload the readings. Two hours of training, one Saturday a season.

No waders required — the marks are on bridges and walls, and we never send anyone into moving water. Ever.
