Certified dark, on purpose

In 2019 the ridge was measured at 21.7 magnitudes per square arcsecond — dark enough to read star charts by the Milky Way, almost.

Keeping it that way is a daily practice, not a plaque. Every exterior light on the property is amber and shielded. The path lights go out at nine. The nearest town agreed to downward-facing lamps in 2021, after we brought their council up here one October night and simply pointed up.

Your host, Dr. Amara Osei, ran the university observatory in the valley for fourteen years. She still gives the 10 p.m. sky tour when the clouds allow — which here is roughly 280 nights a year.

New-moon weekends (Aug 2–4, Aug 30–Sep 1) are typically gone eight weeks out. Weeknights around them are kinder to spontaneous people.

What to bring, honestly

  • Real layers. It is 4°C at the telescope field at 2 a.m., even in August. Bring the fleece you think you won’t need. You will need it.
  • Patience for your own eyes. Full dark adaptation takes thirty minutes. Every phone glance resets the clock.
  • Food you like. The cabins have proper kitchens; the nearest store is forty minutes down the mountain.
  • Nothing that glows white. No white flashlights, no car interior lights left on, no exceptions after 21:00. Red light only — we provide the torches.
