Why it doesn't swish

Most waterproof shells are loud because the membrane is laminated to a slick face fabric — every arm move is a garbage-bag crunch. Deer hear that at forty yards on a still morning. So do turkeys.

Our shell face is brushed tricot: the surface is a dense, soft pile that kills the crinkle before it starts. The membrane sits behind it, so you keep the 20k/20k rating without the soundtrack.

The test we actually use: hang the jacket in a dead-calm barn, have someone swing their arms at three meters. If you can hear it, it doesn't ship. Our current batch is audible at about one meter, which is quieter than your own breathing after the walk in.

Care is boring, on purpose: machine wash cold, no softener, line dry. The DWR renews with ten minutes in a warm dryer.

Lifetime repairs, no asterisks

You rip it, we fix it. That's the whole policy, but here are the details people ask about.

What we fix free, forever

  • Seams and stitching — blown seams, loose bar tacks, unraveling hems
  • Zippers and snaps — replaced with the same hardware, or better if the old stock ran out
  • Tears — barbed wire, blackthorn, the fence you swore you could step over
  • Delamination — if the membrane lets go, that's on us, full stop

How it works

Wash it first — the bench crew votes on this every year and it stays in the policy. Print the repair form, box it up, send it to Kettle Falls. Back in your hands in about three weeks. If it's your only shell and it's mid-season, say so on the form: we keep a rack of loaner jackets for exactly that.

The record

The current record is a 1994 Kettle shell repaired eleven times — same owner, same jacket, three states of hunting seasons. It's more patch than original at this point and he wouldn't trade it for a new one. Neither would we.

Repairs are free. You pay shipping one way; we pay it back. That has been the deal since 1987 and it isn't changing.
