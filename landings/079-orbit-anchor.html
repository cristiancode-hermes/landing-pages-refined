Learn to sail. Actually sail.

Orbit & Anchor is a working sailing school on Kilbride Harbor. We teach on real boats, in real weather, and we don't hand you a certificate for sitting through a slideshow. You'll be heeled over and grinning by your second lesson — or wondering what you signed up for, which is also fine.

Kilbride Harbor · Est. 1998 · Lessons April–October

First, learn where the wind is

Everything in sailing starts with the points of sail. Pick one — the boat turns, and we'll tell you what it feels like out there.

Close-hauled

~45° off the wind

Heeled hard, spray in your teeth, everything trimmed tight. The boat feels alive and slightly annoyed. Week two of Skipper I you'll hate it; week three it'll be why you come back.

Instructors make you call the point of sail before every tack for the entire first course. By the end you do it in your sleep. That's the idea.

The certification ladder

Three rungs. Each one unlocks more of the fleet, and each one ends with you alone at the helm while the instructor pretends to read a magazine.

  1. I

    Skipper I — Keelboat basics

    12 hours over 4 sessions · includes the capsize drill · max 4 students per boat

    Points of sail, tacking, gybing, docking without tears, and what every rope on the boat is actually called. You'll crew first, helm second, and by the fourth session you'll rig Meadowlark yourself while we watch with coffee.

    $480
  2. II

    Skipper II — Coastal confident

    18 hours incl. one full-day sail · chart work, anchoring, man-overboard until it's boring

    Now the harbor opens up. Tides, channels, anchoring for lunch, reefing before you need to, and recovering a fender you dropped because you will drop a fender. Skipper II holders can take the J/24s anywhere inside the bay.

    $690
  3. III

    Skipper III — Night & weather

    24 hours incl. two night sails · spinnaker endorsement optional · by invitation after II

    Lights, shipping lanes, weather calls you can't take back, and sailing by feel when the horizon is gone. The spinnaker add-on teaches you to fly the big red kite without wrapping it around the forestay — most people manage it by the third hoist.

    $920

About the capsize drill

Let's get this out of the way: you will capsize. On purpose. In week two. Every Skipper I student, every season, no exceptions for triathletes, navy veterans, or people who "promise to be careful."

Here's the honesty part. The harbor is 14°C in May and not much warmer in June. We provide wetsuits that mostly fit. You will be cold, you will swallow some harbor, and the boat will turtle further than you expected the first time. Nobody has ever enjoyed the drill. Everybody has passed it.

Why we insist: the first unplanned capsize — and it comes for every sailor — should happen in a body of water you already know, next to people who've fished out four hundred students. Fear of capsizing ruins more sailors than capsizing ever has.

Afterwards there's hot soup in the clubhouse and your name goes on the Turtled List on the wall, which by now is longer than the membership roster. Wear it proudly.

The bowline, forever

One knot to start with. It doesn't slip, it doesn't jam, and you can untie it with frozen fingers. Watch it tie itself, then learn it on a real line at the dock.

  1. 1Make the loop — the rabbit hole, small end toward you.
  2. 2Rabbit comes up — working end up through the hole.
  3. 3Around the tree — behind the standing part, the long way round.
  4. 4Back down the hole — snug it home. That's the whole knot.

The fleet

Five boats, all named by students, all with opinions. What you can rent depends on your rung — the boats don't care how good you say you are.

Beagle

6.7 m keelboat · 4 souls · the school bus Skipper I

Forgives everything, points like a shopping cart, impossible to embarrass. More sailors have learned on Beagle than on every other boat in the harbor combined.

Meadowlark

7.3 m · 5 souls · Beagle's quicker sister Skipper I

Same manners, better legs. This is the one in the photo at the top, heeled over with three students who swore they weren't nervous.

Kestrel

J/24 · 4 souls · the honest one Skipper II

Tells you immediately when your trim is wrong, in a language of weather helm and lost speed. Graduates of Skipper II either love Kestrel or respect her from the dock.

Nightjar

8.8 m · 6 souls · cabin, stove, opinions Skipper III

The overnight boat. Anchor in Seal Cove, cook something ambitious on the two-burner, and learn that everything sounds bigger in the dark.

Redshift

9.1 m · 6 souls · the big red kite Skipper III + spinnaker

Carries the spinnaker the school is named for — the red one you've seen from the ferry. Fast, dramatic, and utterly unforgiving of a lazy foredeck.
