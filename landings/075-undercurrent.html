The Path Down

Three rungs, in order, no skipping. Every course is capped at four students per instructor, because buoyancy is personal.

  1. Open Water Diver

    4 days · $545 all-in · max depth 18 m

    Two pool sessions, four ocean dives, one very patient instructor. You'll flood and clear your mask until it's boring, which is exactly the point. Includes all gear, the e-learning, and the certification card.

  2. Advanced Open Water

    2 days · $395 · max depth 30 m

    Five adventure dives: deep, navigation, night, drift, and peak-performance buoyancy. This is where you stop swimming like a windmill and start hovering like you mean it.

  3. Rescue Diver

    3 days · $470 · the serious one

    Panicked-diver scenarios, tired-diver tows, missing-diver searches. It's exhausting and it's the course our instructors respect most. EFR first-aid included; bring snacks and humility.

Five Sites, Honestly Rated

We don't call everything "suitable for all levels," because it isn't. Here's what each site actually asks of you.

Kohanaiki Gardens

8–12 m

Our classroom reef. Gentle slope, friendly turtles, surge mild enough for a first ocean dive. If you can't relax here, the ocean is telling you something.

Beginner — where every Open Water course starts

Turtle Pinnacle

12–18 m

A cleaning station where green turtles queue up like customers. Mild current on the outgoing tide; keep your buoyancy tidy near the coral or the divemaster will have words.

Beginner+ — comfortable with 6 dives logged

Manta Night Dive

10 m

You kneel in a circle with a light pointed up; mantas with four-meter wingspans do barrel rolls inches over your head. Shallow and stationary, but night water rattles some people — say so beforehand, no shame in it.

Intermediate — night comfort required

The Cathedral

18–28 m

A lava-tube swim-through with a skylight that does exactly what the name promises. Overhead environment, single-file, no touching the ceiling — silt-outs are real and we brief them seriously.

Advanced — AOW with solid buoyancy, 20+ dives

The Washing Machine

22–40 m

Named by a divemaster who got rolled in its surge channel in 2014. Deep wall, ripping current on the wrong tide, hammerheads on the right one. We run it maybe ten days a year, and we cancel without apology.

Expert only — Rescue-level skills, 50+ dives, checkout dive required

The Record, Unedited

Since 2009 we've guided 4,200-some dives and taught 1,100 students. Zero serious injuries. Zero chamber rides. That number matters to us more than any five-star rating, and we protect it by being boring: conservative profiles, nitrox analysis on camera, and a standing rule that any guide can cancel any dive for any reason with zero questions from the front desk.

The honest footnotes: one bent D-ring (2016), one lost wedding band (recovered, 2019), one diver stung by a Portuguese man o' war on the surface interval (treated on board, finished his holiday). We'll tell you all three stories on the boat if you ask.
