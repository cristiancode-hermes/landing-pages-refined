Four weeks before you throw a hard right hand.

That's not a sales line — it's the program. Everybody starts at the same place, including the ex-college athletes who are sure they don't need to. The ones who listen get good fast.

  1. Stance and balance. Feet under you, hands home, chin down. You'll stand in front of a mirror and feel silly. That's the work.

  2. Footwork. Step-drag, pivots, never crossing your feet. Most beginners lose every exchange before a punch is thrown — this is why.

  3. The jab. One whole week on the most important punch in boxing. Range, timing, the double. You'll dream about it.

  4. The cross, finally. Hip first, fist second. By the end of week four you'll hit the bag harder than the loud guy from your first night — correctly.

Two tracks. Same gym, same respect.

Pick the one that fits your life. You can switch any time — about a third of the fight team started in open gym swearing they'd never spar.

Fight team

For people who want to compete

Tryouts twice a year, January and September. Six months of fundamentals before your first sanctioned smoker. We register you with USA Boxing, we corner your fights, we tell you honestly when you're ready — and when you're not.

Sparring is supervised, technical, and hard. Current team: 14 active, 3 regional titles this year.

What actually happens

  • Bring: water, flat shoes you can pivot in. We lend gloves and wraps.
  • First 15: jump rope and shadowboxing. Nobody watches; everybody's busy.
  • Next 30: stance, the jab, bag rounds with a coach checking on you.
  • Last 15: conditioning. Scaled to you — nobody keeps score.
  • Never: nobody hits you. Sparring is opt-in, months down the road, if ever.
