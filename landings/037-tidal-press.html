Registered to the hair

Two colours means two passes through the press, and the second has to land exactly on the first. Watch the ampersand: that's what misregistration looks like, and that's it snapping into register — a tolerance of a quarter millimetre, checked by eye on every fiftieth sheet.

The registration marks never make it onto your finished piece. They're trimmed away and recycled, like all our offcuts.

Getting a quote is three emails, not a funnel

Send us the words

Email your text, rough quantities, and any deadline. A photo of something you loved helps. Within 48 hours you get a price and an honest answer on timing — usually 3 to 4 weeks out.

Proof within five working days

We typeset your job and send a digital proof, then one physical press proof in the post for anything over £300. You check spelling with a pencil, not a hope.

On press, then in the post

Every plate is imaged from a 600 dpi film negative — the 600 dpi promise we make in writing. Your job ships tracked, flat-packed between boards, with two spare proofs to keep.

Slow on purpose

A digital shop can turn your cards around in a day. We can't, and we won't pretend otherwise. What we can do is put an impression into the paper that your thumbs will find before your eyes do — the thing that makes someone turn a card over and ask where it came from.

We take on twelve wedding suites a season and a handful of commercial jobs beside. When the book is full, the website says so, and we mean it.
