A café for readers on Exmouth Street. Espresso pulled slowly, fiction shelved short, and no Wi-Fi — on purpose. Bring the book you're stuck on. Leave further in.

Today's pairing What happens at night

Espresso, with a story on the side.

Every drink comes with a short story, printed that morning and matched to the roast. Finishing one before your cup goes cold is considered good form.

  • Ethiopia Guji, pour-over £4.50 paired with “The Locksmith of Trieste” — 900 words, seven minutes, one bad decision
  • Flat white £3.80 paired with “Instructions for a Power Cut” — 400 words, best read by candlelight
  • Batch filter, bottomless until ten £3.00 paired with the crossword — unsolved since Tuesday, clues disputed
  • Hot chocolate, the thick kind £4.20 paired with a poem. Always a poem. We don't make the rules — actually, we do

After the last chapter, we turn the chairs down.

Two nights a month the café stays open late. Tickets are free; the rule is you have to stay for the whole thing.

Third Thursday of the month · 19:30

Margins — an open mic for unfinished work

Bring the draft you can't end. Read for five minutes, then strangers tell you what they think happens next. The ending with the most votes gets written on the wall.

First Sunday of the month · 18:00–20:00

The Silent Reading Party

Phones in the basket by the door. Two hours of collective reading, one viola at seven, soup at eight. The quietest ticket in the city, and the hardest to get.
