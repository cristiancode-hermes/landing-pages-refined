The ritual of the hour

In Milan the aperitivo is not a drink, it's an appointment. The piazza fills, the light goes long and orange, and the first spritz arrives with a little plate of something salty. We made Vermouth Hours for that exact window — and wrote its schedule on the label, because rituals deserve timetables.

  • 17:45Glasses out of the freezer. Yes, the freezer.
  • 18:00First pour. Phones face-down.
  • 18:20Olives, crisps, a small argument about football.
  • 19:00Second pour, or dinner. Both correct.

Three ways to keep the hour

All three start the same way: a cold glass and no distractions.

  • The Piazza.

    60 ml Vermouth Hours, 90 ml prosecco, 30 ml soda. Built over ice in a big wine glass, orange slice dropped in last so it floats like a little sun. The one in the photograph, the one on the label.

    €7 at the bar
  • The Balcony.

    75 ml Vermouth Hours over one large cube, nothing else. For when the bottle just came home and the freezer glass is still foggy. Bitter first, then orange peel, then a slow vanilla finish.

    €5 at the bar
  • The Long Evening.

    50 ml Vermouth Hours, 150 ml tonic, a rosemary sprig slapped awake. Lower, longer, built for the second hour when dinner keeps not happening and nobody minds.

    €6.50 at the bar
