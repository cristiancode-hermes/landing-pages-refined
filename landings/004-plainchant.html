Three Ales, Brewed by the Hours

Each ale is named for the office at which it is racked. We brew what the barley allows and the abbot permits, in that order.

The Rule of the Brewery

Chapter nine of the house rule concerns the brewhouse. It is read aloud each Brewsday. Extracts follow.

Rule the First

The kettle shall be scraped, for residue is pride, and pride is off-flavours.

Rule the Second

Silence is kept from mashing-in until the wort is cooled. Humming is permitted. Whistling is not, for it frightens the yeast.

Rule the Third

No brother shall taste from the conditioning cask before the appointed day, however persuasive his thirst. Brother Martin.

Rule the Fourth

When the barley is poor, give thanks; when the barley is good, give more thanks and write down what you did.

Rule the Fifth

The first bottle of every batch goes to the chapel roof fund. The roof has been leaking since 1342 and will continue to do so, but the gesture is the thing.

The Cellar Door

Our shop is the low door beneath the chapter house, down eleven worn steps. Bring a stout bag and a soft voice; the door is low for a reason, and the reason is humility.

We sell bottles, wooden tokens for the winter market, and a small pamphlet of brewing psalms. We do not sell glasses, for drinking from the bottle builds character.
