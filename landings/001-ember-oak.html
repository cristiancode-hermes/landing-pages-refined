Dough is a patient thing. We don't rush it.

Every ball of dough at Ember & Oak starts two days before you sit down. Caputo flour, water, sea salt, a whisper of starter — then forty-eight hours cold, slow, and undisturbed in the basement. It comes up to room temperature at noon and meets the fire at five.

What lands on your table is leopard-spotted, soft in the middle, crisp at the rim — the way a pizza behaves when the oven is hot enough to be a little scary.

The menu, short on purpose

Six pizzas, one oven, no substitutions on the margherita — we tried it your way in 2016 and we're still sorry.

  • Margherita the benchmark San Marzano tomato, fior di latte pulled this morning, basil from the planter box out front, cold-pressed olive oil. $14
  • Marinara No cheese, no apology. Tomato, garlic confit, oregano, and the oven doing all the talking. Vegan as it stands. $13
  • Funghi Roasted maitake and cremini, taleggio, thyme, a little truffle oil — applied after the bake, where it belongs. $17
  • Diavola brings heat Spicy soppressata, Calabrian chili honey, smoked mozzarella. The honey goes on last and it matters. $18
  • Mortadella e Pistacchio Bianca base, mortadella laid on after the fire, crushed Sicilian pistachios, lemon zest, stracciatella. $19

Gluten-free dough is made in-house on Mondays — ask your server. Everything cuts into eight slices unless you say otherwise.

Forty seats, one long table

The room is small on purpose. There's the communal oak table down the middle — reclaimed from a barn outside Salem — a handful of two-tops along the window, and four stools at the oven counter where you can watch your dinner blister.

We keep a short list of natural wines and three local beers on tap. The soundtrack is whatever the kitchen is arguing about. Fridays, it's usually funk.

When the fire's lit

We bake until the dough runs out — some Fridays that's 9:40, some it's midnight. If the oak is stacked by the door, we're open. Walk-ins always welcome at the oven counter.
