Spot a fake before you fund one

Half the "1994" shirts on the big resale sites were sewn last year in a different country. The free checks we run on every shirt that comes through our door:

  • Wash-label country. A '94 shirt made somewhere the brand didn't use until 2003 is a remake, whatever the seller's story.
  • Sponsor texture. Original felt and flock sponsors crack with age in straight stress lines. Printed-on fakes crack in a spiderweb — or not at all.
  • Stitch count. Overlock seams on originals run tight and even. Wobbly, wide-spaced stitching is the loudest tell there is.
  • The smell test. Genuinely thirty-year-old cotton smells of loft, not factory. New "vintage" smells of dye and plasticiser.

Sell us the shirts in your loft

We buy outright — no consignment, no waiting for a buyer. The whole thing usually takes a week, and we pay the same day we authenticate.

Send photos

Front, back, wash label, and the sponsor up close. Email them over — a phone camera by a window is plenty. We'll tell you within two days if it's our sort of stock.

Get an offer

A straight cash number based on the last twelve months of real sales, not "whatever eBay says this week". You're free to say no; about a fifth of people do.

Post or drop in

We cover insured postage, or bring it to the shop and watch it go under the lamp yourself. Payment lands the same day it passes — bank transfer, cash if you're local.
