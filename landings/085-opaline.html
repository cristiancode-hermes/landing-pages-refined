A perfume is a pyramid that collapses beautifully — the bright head burns off in minutes, the heart carries the afternoon, the base is what your scarf remembers in January. Each pyramid below builds the way the scent does.

The nose

Maëlle Varenne was born in Grasse in 1963, in an apartment above a jasmine processor. She trained at ISIPCA, spent nine years composing for houses she is contractually forbidden to name, and came home in 1987 to found Opaline with one rule: nothing ships until she would wear it herself for a year.

She still smells every batch. The house releases nothing on a schedule — Figuier de Minuit took seven years, Pluie d'Opale eleven, Or Fumé four. A fourth fragrance has been "almost ready" since 2019. She is in no hurry, and neither are we.

“A perfume is finished when removing anything makes it worse, not when adding something makes it better.”

— Maëlle Varenne, in conversation with the house journal, 2021
