The fitting comes first. The frame follows.

Ninety minutes on the fit bike before we talk about a single tube. We measure your current position, your flexibility, and the riding you actually do — not the riding you put on Instagram.

  1. The interview

    Where you ride, how far, what hurts. Most frame problems are fit problems wearing a costume.

  2. Ninety minutes on the jig

    Saddle height, setback, reach, drop — dialed while you pedal under load. You leave with your numbers either way.

  3. Geometry drawn to you

    Your position becomes stack, reach, and angles. We send the drawing before we cut anything. Changes are free at this stage — after the torch, they’re not.

Steel or titanium — the honest version.

Both are excellent. Neither is magic. Here’s what we tell people across the bench, with no horse in the race beyond your riding.

Columbus steel

Frameset from $3,400

The ride
Lively and springy under a strong rider; the classic feel, because the classics were right.
The weight
A finished 56 cm frame lands around 1,650 g. Roughly 350 g heavier than ti. On a 9 kg bike, you will not feel it.
The catch
It rusts if you neglect it. Frame-saver spray once a year, wipe it down after winter rides, and it outlives you anyway.
Choose it if
You want the best ride-per-dollar in cycling and a frame that any shop on earth can repair.

Grade 9 titanium

Frameset from $4,900

The ride
Smooth over chatter, quiet on long days. Slightly more forgiving than steel without going soft.
The weight
Around 1,300 g for a 56. Real, but smaller than the internet claims.
The catch
Costs $1,500 more, takes three extra weeks at the welder, and a cracked ti frame is a specialist repair, not a roadside one.
Choose it if
You ride in salt air or wet winters, hate paint maintenance, or plan to keep one frame for thirty years.

Fourteen weeks, week by week.

We publish the queue because you deserve to know where your deposit sits. Here’s exactly what those weeks contain.

Week 1

Deposit & fitting

$500 holds your slot. Fitting happens at the shop or remotely over video with your measurements.

Weeks 2–3

Drawing & sign-off

Geometry drawn, sent to you, revised until it’s right. Nothing is cut before your written approval.

Weeks 4–6

Tubes selected & mitered

Tube diameters and wall thicknesses chosen for your weight and use. Mitered by hand, checked on the surface plate.

Weeks 7–9

Brazed & aligned

Brass brazing over two days, then cold-set on the alignment table until every face is true to half a millimeter.

Weeks 10–12

Finish

Paint in-house (steel) or media-blast and anodized graphics (ti). Cures for a full week — no shortcuts here.

Weeks 13–14

Assembly & handover

Faced, chased, and built with your parts or ours. You collect it in Bellingham or it ships in a reusable crate.

Current queue: taking deposits for October 2026 builds. If we slip past week 14, we tell you the week it happens and why — and the finish work is on us.
