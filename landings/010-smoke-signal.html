The Pit Never Lies

Marla "Two-Alarm" Reyes has minded the same 1,000-gallon offset since 1998. She stokes the first coals at 4 a.m., reads the smoke by color — thin and blue or nothing — and won't let anybody else wrap the briskets. The paper's pink, the twine's cotton, and that's the whole recipe.

We burn post oak split on the property, about a cord and a half a week. No gas assist, no pellet hopper, no thermometer with a battery in it. If the fire's wrong, the meat waits. So do you, and it'll be worth it.

Line Etiquette

The line forms at the oak fence and moves faster than it looks. Mind a few rules and everybody eats happy.

  • Rule the first
    Hold your spot, not your whole group's. One body in line per tray you're buying. Saving space for seven cousins who left for breakfast tacos is how fights start.
  • Rule the second
    Know your order by the time you hit the counter. It's three meats and three sides, friend. The menu's been on this page the whole time.
  • Rule the third
    First-timers get a burnt end while they wait. Tell Marla it's your first visit and she'll cut you one off the point. Tipping it back is considered poor manners.
  • Rule the fourth
    "Sold out" means sold out. The stamp doesn't negotiate. Come earlier tomorrow — the pit's been lit since four, same as ever.
