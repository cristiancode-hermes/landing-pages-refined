The station that refused to close

Grand Terminal opened on the first of May, 1912, with a glass roof the papers called "the largest room in the North." It survived two wars, the Beeching cuts, and a 1974 proposal to replace the façade with something made of concrete and regret.

When the last scheduled service left in 1989, the building did what stations do: it waited. The restoration took six years. We reopened the booking hall as a lobby, the first-class waiting rooms as bedrooms, and the clock — always the clock — over the restaurant.

ORIGINAL FIXTURES KEPT: THE CLOCK (1912), THE TICKET WINDOWS (NOW THE BAR BACK), 4,100 PANES OF ROOF GLASS, AND ONE GHOST, UNVERIFIED.

The Buffer Stop — dinner under the clock

The restaurant sits where the buffers once stood, directly beneath the great clock. The menu is set, the pace is not; nobody has ever missed a train because of our cheese course, but two people have missed one on purpose.

The Trolley — drinks, platform side

The bar is built around the station's original luggage trolley, which carried mail sacks for forty years and now carries better cargo. Cocktails are named for destinations the line no longer serves.

Order a Millgate Junction (rye, amaro, coal-smoke bitters) and sit by the glass as the 23:40 sleeper pulls in. Last orders are at 00:42 — when the last train leaves, so does the barman.
