Harrow Court

Kent · 0.8 ha · planted 2022

Hotwells Courtyard

Bristol · 180 m² · planted 2023

A lightwell between two Georgian terraces, four metres across at its narrowest. Five multi-stem Amelanchier hold the vertical space; everything below them is shade-tolerant, slow, and chosen to look deliberate in February.

The five-year honesty

Photographs of new gardens lie. Ours looked sparse the day we planted them — every one. A parterre at one litre per plant is a drawing, not a garden, and we tell clients so before they sign.

Year three is when structure arrives. Year five is when the planting reads as we intended, and it is also when the maintenance regime — not the design — decides whether it stays that way. That is why our fee includes five years of seasonal visits, and why we turn down clients who plan to hand the garden to nobody.

From survey to planting

  1. Survey and soil

    Two weeks. Topographic survey, soil pits at one per hundred square metres, and a season of watching where water and frost actually sit.

  2. Concept plan

    One drawing, argued over. We present a single scheme — not three options — because a garden needs a conviction, not a menu.

  3. Technical design

    Setting-out plans, grading, drainage, and the planting schedule with every plant counted and sourced from named nurseries.

  4. Planting

    We plant with our own small crew, bare-root where the season allows. Structure plants first, infill the following autumn.

  5. Aftercare

    Five years of seasonal visits: pruning, re-edging, honest replacement of what died, and a written note after each visit.
