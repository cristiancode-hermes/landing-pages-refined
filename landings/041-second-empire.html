The condition scale, in plain terms

Every piece gets a grade before it gets a price. We grade harshly; most dealers' "excellent" is our 3.

Grade 5 — Museum

As close to the workshop door as an old object gets. We've graded eleven pieces a 5 in fifteen years. You probably can't afford it; neither can we.

Grade 4 — Loved, carefully

Used by someone who owned coasters. Minor wear in the right places. The pharmacy lamp this week is a 4.

Grade 3 — Honest

Visible history: fade, a ring, a dent with a story. Structurally perfect, cosmetically human. Most of what we sell, and most of what you should buy.

Grade 2 — Project

Needs work we'll name precisely: a re-glue, a re-cane, a veneer patch. Priced so the work makes sense. We tell you exactly what Tomas would charge to do it.

Grade 1 — As found

Straight from the estate, dust included. Sold fast and cheap to people who know what they're looking at. We don't deliver Grade 1; you take it away yourself.

If a piece arrives and you think we graded it generously, bring it back within 14 days for a full refund. That has happened twice. Both times we changed the grade and thanked the customer.

Delivery, and the stairs policy

We deliver ourselves, in the van, with blankets and Tomas. We don't use couriers for anything heavier than the lamp.

What it costs

  • $90 — ground floor, within 40 km of the shop
  • +$35 per flight — a flight is 12 stairs; we count together on the day
  • No elevator credit — if it fits in the lift, the stairs are free; that's the deal
  • $140 — beyond 40 km, plus fuel at cost, quoted before you commit

Before the van leaves

We measure your doorframes, your hallway turn, and your staircase before we load anything — a free visit within the city, folded into the delivery fee beyond it. If the credenza doesn't fit your turn, you find out on Tuesday, not on Saturday with the van idling outside.

On the day

Two people, felt pads, and the piece placed where you want it — we'll move it three times without sighing. Packaging comes back with us. If we scratch your floor, we pay for the repair; we've budgeted for zero scratches and spent the budget once, in 2017.
