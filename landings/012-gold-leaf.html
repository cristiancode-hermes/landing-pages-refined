Taste the Collection How We Gild

One valley. One harvest. Nothing else in the bar.

Our cacao comes from a single cooperative of fourteen families along the Ucayali River in Peru. The beans travel once, by boat and then by rail, and arrive in Lyon still smelling of the fermentation boxes — banana, black plum, wet earth.

We pay 2.4× the commodity price, fixed for five harvests, so the cooperative can plan grafting cycles instead of chasing spot markets. You taste the difference as a long, quiet finish instead of a bitter shout.

The gilding, in four unhurried movements

Every truffle passes through the same four hands. The gold is edible 24-karat leaf, applied sheet by sheet with squirrel-hair brushes — no sprays, no shortcuts.

  1. Temper

    The couverture is brought to 31.2°C by marble and patience. A degree higher and the snap dulls; a degree lower and the shine never sets.

  2. Shell

    Each shell is cast at exactly 1.4 mm — thin enough to crack under a tooth, thick enough to hold a ganache at full humidity.

  3. Fill

    Ganache is piped at 27°C and rested sixteen hours. The crimson-heart batches get a single drop of raspberry distillate at the center.

  4. Gild

    One sheet of gold leaf per truffle, laid by hand. About one in twenty tears — those become the chocolatiers' lunch, never your box.
