How a motion becomes a decision

The same five steps every time, in the open. No back rooms, no "we'll get back to you," no decision that exists only in someone's memory.

  1. Proposed

    Any board member — or any resident with 25 signatures — files a motion. It's public the moment it's filed.

  2. Seconded

    A second member puts it on the next agenda. Agendas go out 72 hours before the meeting, by law and by default.

  3. Discussed

    Residents comment online or in person. Every comment is attached to the motion, attributed and searchable.

  4. Decided

    The vote is tallied as it's cast — in the room, on the livestream, on the public page. Same numbers everywhere.

  5. Published

    Minutes, tally and full record publish within 24 hours. The median across our towns is 22 minutes.

The record belongs to the town

Not to us, and not behind a login. Every agenda, motion, vote and set of minutes is public by default and exportable in full — as CSV, as JSON, or as a plain web page anyone can read.

  • No login to read. If your town uses Quorum, its record is on the open web. Residents, journalists, researchers — same access.
  • One-click export, everything. A clerk can hand over the entire archive on a thumb drive in an afternoon.
  • A boring, documented format. If we ever shut down, the export keeps working. We publish the schema.
  • Never sold, never mined. We charge towns a published price. That is the whole business model.
