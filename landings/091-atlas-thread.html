Three fittings, no shortcuts between them

Bespoke means the garment exists between fittings, not before them. Each stage answers a question the last one raised.

  1. The skeleton baste

    Week 4 · about an hour

    You try the coat held together by white basting thread — no buttonholes, no lining, no pockets. We pin the balance, mark the shoulders in chalk, and take the whole thing apart again in front of you. That’s the point: nothing is precious yet.

  2. The forward fitting

    Week 7 · about forty minutes

    Now it has sleeves, a collar, and the beginnings of a chest. We check how it moves when you reach for a shelf, sit down, check a phone. Most of what we adjust here is what you do, not what you measure.

  3. The finish

    Week 10 · as long as it takes

    Buttonholes sewn by hand — the left lapel gets the crimson one, our signature since the shop opened. Final press, sleeve pitch checked one last time, and you wear it out of the shop if the weather allows.

Alterations for life

Bodies change; the coat should be allowed to. Gain or lose a stone, take up running, stop — bring it back and we re-cut it on your pattern, free, for as long as the cloth holds. Our record is a 1971 tweed still in rotation on its third owner in the family.
